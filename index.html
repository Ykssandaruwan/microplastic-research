Built around four scientific questions

The structure follows a simple research logic: where microplastics come from, how they are measured, what happens to them after release and why they matter for people and ecosystems.

What are they?

Microplastics are small plastic particles and fibres formed either intentionally or through the breakdown of larger plastic materials.

How are they detected?

Methods include microscopy, FTIR, Raman, thermal analysis, fluorescence screening, electrical sensing and AI-assisted classification.

How do they transform?

Sunlight, abrasion, heat, microbes and chemical interactions can change particle size, surface chemistry, density and transport behaviour.

Why health matters?

Research focuses on exposure routes, particle behaviour, additives, inflammation, oxidative stress and the uncertainty in human risk assessment.

A careful scientific position

Microplastic exposure is real, but scientific confidence differs across matrices and health outcomes. Stronger methods, clean sampling, transparent reporting and cross-validation are needed before results can be compared confidently across studies.

This website therefore avoids exaggerated claims. It presents the current evidence as an evolving scientific field with important knowledge gaps.
